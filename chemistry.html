Raise the CO₂ in the air above the sea and watch what the water does, step by step.

Pump in CO₂

CO₂ reacts with carbonate ions: CO₂ + CO₃²⁻ + H₂O → 2 HCO₃⁻. Carbonate, which shells are built from, gets used up and hydrogen ions build up.

Change against pre-industrial water

Bars use a compressed scale so small and large changes both stay visible. In the sea these steps happen together; they are shown one at a time for clarity, and every number comes from the model's solution at the new CO₂ level (18 °C, default alkalinity). Pre-industrial here is the model's 278 ppm baseline, slightly below the 285 ppm record for 1850.

How the model solves it

1

Air meets water

Surface water takes up a set share of the rise in atmospheric CO₂. That share, 89%, is calibrated to the observed 1985–2022 pH trend.

2

Solve for H⁺

Given alkalinity and CO₂, the model searches for the hydrogen-ion concentration that balances carbonate, borate and water charges.

3

Read the outputs

pH, dissolved inorganic carbon, carbonate, and Ω: calcium × carbonate over aragonite's solubility product.
