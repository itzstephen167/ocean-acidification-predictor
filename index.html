03 · Under the hood

Why more CO₂ means less carbonate.

CO₂ reacts with water and with carbonate ions: CO₂ + CO₃²⁻ + H₂O → 2 HCO₃⁻. Carbonate, the ion shells are built from, gets used up and hydrogen ions pile up. Slide the year in the explorer to see how each ingredient has shifted relative to pre-industrial water.

1 · Air meets water

Surface water takes up a fraction of the rise in atmospheric CO₂ (calibrated: 89% to match the observed 1985–2022 pH trend).

2 · Solve for [H⁺]

Given alkalinity and CO₂, the model bisects on hydrogen-ion concentration until charge balance holds across carbonate, borate and water.

3 · Read the outputs

pH, dissolved inorganic carbon, carbonate, and Ω: calcium × carbonate over the aragonite solubility product.

04 · Honest limits

What this model is, and isn't.

Checks it passes

  • Calibrated to one number only: the 1985–2022 trend of −0.0017 pH/yr (Copernicus Marine).
  • Untuned check: pH change 1850→2022 is −0.125, close to the published ≈ −0.1.
  • Untuned check: Revelle factor 10.6, inside the typical 8–14 range.
  • Very-high scenario lands at pH 7.70 in 2100, in line with published high-emissions projections.

Where to be careful

  • Global-mean surface box. Cold, upwelling and coastal waters sit well away from 8.04.
  • The shaded band is parameter uncertainty only (alkalinity ±40, temperature ±1.5 °C, equilibration ±0.05). It excludes scenario choice, regional differences and natural variability.
  • Not modelled: changing alkalinity or circulation, biology, sulfate/fluoride/phosphate/silicate terms.
  • CO₂ history is an approximation of the Mauna Loa record; scenarios are SSP-like. Swap in official files before quoting numbers in anything formal.

Can a sensor even see this?

The repo includes a potentiometric-electrode simulator. One robust result: an electrode drift of just 0.1 mV per year mimics the entire global acidification trend. Catching a −0.0017 pH/yr signal takes careful calibration, not just a good sensor.

Sensor noise and drift values are simulation inputs, not specs of any real product.
