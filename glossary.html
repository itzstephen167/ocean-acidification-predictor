Glossary

The terms used across the site, in plain language.

Terms

14 entries

pH Scale
A measure of how acidic water is. Lower means more acidic. The scale is logarithmic: a drop of 0.1 means about 26% more hydrogen ions. The model puts today's average surface ocean near 8.04, down from about 8.17 in 1850. See it in the explorer
Hydrogen ion (H⁺) Chemistry
The particle that makes water acidic. pH is the negative logarithm of its concentration, so pH 8 means 10⁻⁸ moles per kilogram. Watch it build up
Ocean acidification Process
The fall in seawater pH as the ocean absorbs CO₂ from the air. The sea stays above pH 7, so it is still slightly alkaline; "acidification" describes the direction of change. Follow it from 1850
ppm (parts per million) Unit
How CO₂ in the air is counted: molecules of CO₂ per million molecules of dry air. It was about 285 ppm in 1850 and is above 420 ppm today.
pCO₂ Chemistry
The partial pressure of CO₂: the share of total gas pressure that CO₂ provides, in microatmospheres (µatm). In the model, surface water takes up 89% of the rise in atmospheric CO₂, so seawater pCO₂ follows the air closely. See how the model works
Dissolved inorganic carbon (DIC) Chemistry
All the carbon dissolved in seawater from CO₂, added together: dissolved CO₂, bicarbonate and carbonate. Roughly 90% of it is bicarbonate. See the three forms
Bicarbonate (HCO₃⁻) Chemistry
The most common form of dissolved carbon in the sea. When CO₂ reacts with a carbonate ion and water, two bicarbonate ions are produced. See the reaction
Carbonate ion (CO₃²⁻) Chemistry
The building block that corals, shellfish and some plankton combine with calcium to make shells and skeletons. Added CO₂ uses it up, which is why acidification makes shell-building harder. Watch it get used up
Total alkalinity Setting
The seawater's capacity to neutralise acid, in micromoles per kilogram (µmol/kg). Waters with higher alkalinity hold a higher pH for the same CO₂. The model's default is 2300, and the explorer lets you change it. Change it in the explorer
Aragonite Mineral
A form of calcium carbonate used by corals, pteropods and many shellfish. It dissolves more easily than calcite, the other common form, so it is the usual early-warning mineral.
Saturation state (Ω) Output
Calcium concentration times carbonate concentration, divided by aragonite's solubility product. Above 1, aragonite is stable; below 1, unprotected shells start to dissolve. Many organisms are stressed well before Ω reaches 1. See the Ω chart
Revelle factor Check
How strongly seawater resists taking up more CO₂: the percentage change in pCO₂ divided by the percentage change in DIC. Typical values are 8 to 14, and it rises as the sea takes up CO₂. The model gives 10.6 without being tuned to it. See the other checks
Emissions scenario Input
A possible future path for atmospheric CO₂. The four here (low, intermediate, high, very high) are shaped like the SSP pathways used in climate reports, and are not the official files. Compare two scenarios
Uncertainty range (5–95%) Output
The shaded band on the charts. It shows how much the answer moves when the model's parameters are varied within their plausible ranges. It leaves out scenario choice, regional differences and natural variability. Read the cautions
